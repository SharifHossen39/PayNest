# Editorial Prose Review — PayNest Solution Design

**Purpose/audience read:** This document exists to help a human intermediate Java learner implement PayNest while understanding why its Spring Boot, JPA, PostgreSQL, security, and transaction decisions fit together.

**Voice to preserve:** Direct, technically precise, and deliberately prescriptive. The document uses short guardrails, named Java/Spring constructs, domain-term capitalization, tables, diagrams, and imperative guidance to keep a complex backend build traceable. The edits below preserve that voice and avoid passages the structure review proposes moving or merging unless a surviving sentence still needs clarification.

## Summary

- **Recommendations:** 19 material prose fixes.
- **Baseline:** 8,482 words from the prescribed word-metrics script.
- **Estimated word impact:** approximately 35 fewer words, less than 0.5% of the document. The goal is lower cognitive load, not shortening.
- **Highest-value fixes:** repair the row-locking grammar error; make rollback rules actionable; clarify the dependency-direction explanation; replace ambiguous security wildcard/CORS wording; and make the Phase 13 concurrency gate observable.
- **Structure coordination:** No prose edits target the refinement summary that the structure review moves out of the learner guide. Testing-list prose is left untouched because those lists are slated to merge into roadmap gates. Edits within §8.1 are explicitly limited to details that survive its proposed condensation, and the roadmap edit follows the roadmap to its proposed earlier location.
- **Preserved voice:** The revisions retain the directive technical tone, domain terminology, code identifiers, diagrams, tables, and learner-oriented guardrails.
